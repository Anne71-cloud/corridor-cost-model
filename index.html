What it actually costs to move money between Europe and Africa

The advertised rate is not the cost. Spread, correspondent fees, settlement delay and unhedged exposure during the float are all cost, and only one of them appears on the invoice. This model separates them.

The arithmetic

So you can check it

  • Spread cost = amount × spread ÷ 10,000
  • Fee cost = fixed fees, as entered
  • Delay cost = amount × cost of capital × days ÷ 365
  • All-in cost = spread + fees + delay, as a percentage of the invoice
  • 1σ exposure = amount × volatility × √(days ÷ 365)

Two deliberate simplifications. The delay cost is simple rather than compound interest, which understates it slightly at long delays. The exposure figure assumes returns are roughly normal over a short window — thin-market currencies have fatter tails than that, so treat it as a floor.

What the model leaves out

From the operating side

These are the frictions I see in practice that a calculator can't price, and they are frequently larger than the spread:

  • Repeat KYC and documentary checks on counterparties already onboarded, triggered again by each payment
  • Correspondent banks withdrawing from a corridor with little notice, forcing a rebuild of the payment route
  • Payments held for compliance review with no visibility on when they will clear
  • Stock sitting at port while funds are in flight, which converts a treasury delay into a commercial one
  • The cost of holding buffer balances in a weak currency purely to absorb timing risk

I built this because I run treasury across this corridor and wanted the real number rather than the quoted one. Tell me where the method is wrong — that's more useful to me than agreement.
